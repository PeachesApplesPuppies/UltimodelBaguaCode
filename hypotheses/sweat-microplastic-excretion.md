# Sweat–Microplastic Excretion Hypothesis

**27 November 2026 | Proposed research hypothesis | Untested**

## Research question

Can human eccrine sweating excrete intact microplastic or nanoplastic particles of internal origin, and, if so, does this pathway make a measurable contribution to their elimination from the body?

Sweat contains water, electrolytes and dissolved compounds. Detection of plastic-associated chemicals such as BPA or phthalates is not evidence that intact plastic particles are excreted. Particle transport across sweat gland tissues and into sweat is an unresolved question in this proposal.

## Hypotheses

- H0: No internally derived plastic particles are detectable in sweat above validated detection limits and contamination controls under the tested conditions.
- H1: Internally derived plastic particles are detectably excreted through sweat under the tested conditions.
- Separate follow-up hypothesis: Sweat-mediated particle elimination contributes meaningfully to systemic clearance. Detection alone cannot establish this.

## Proposed laboratory investigation

1. Obtain appropriate ethics approval and use ordinary, supervised physiological sweating without deliberate dehydration, overheating, or exposure to plastic particles.
2. Collect sweat from cleaned skin using validated, low-contamination sampling equipment. Document environmental exposure and all sample-contact materials.
3. Include field, air, skin-surface, equipment and procedural blanks, along with validated recovery controls and paired samples as appropriate. Differentiate gland-derived material from particles already on the skin.
4. Identify and quantify particles using validated size-appropriate methods (e.g., micro-Raman or micro-FTIR for suitable particle sizes, and complementary methods for smaller particles). Polymer mass measurements alone do not establish particle size, intact particle identity or internal origin.
5. Report particle counts, polymer identities, size distributions, sweat volume, detection limits, blank corrections and measurement uncertainty. Replicate findings independently.
6. If internal origin and a reproducible sweat-associated flux can be demonstrated, investigate whether that flux materially changes body burden over time using validated biomarkers and mass-balance methods. Control for ongoing exposure and other elimination pathways.

## Interpretation and falsification

A reproducible signal above controls with evidence of internal origin would support the first hypothesis. A signal explained by surface contamination would not. A well-controlled null result would constrain the hypothesis within the tested conditions and analytical detection limits; it would not prove universal absence.

## Status and safety

This is a research question, not a demonstrated detoxification mechanism or a medical recommendation. No laboratory testing has been performed for this note. Sweating, exercise and sauna use are not established treatments for removing accumulated microplastics. Excessive heat exposure and dehydration can be dangerous.

The proposal is made available for independent investigation by researchers with the appropriate analytical equipment.
