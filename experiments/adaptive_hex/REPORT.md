# Ultimodel Adaptive HEX v0.1 — verification report (2026-11-23)

## Tested result

A new **block-wise adaptive selector** was constructed on top of the earlier HEX 6+2, XOR and bitshuffle transforms and standard zlib/Brotli compressors. It stores per-block transform and backend IDs, exact lengths and checksums. Decoding from the `.uhx` alone reconstructed all test data exactly.

- **28/28 Python regression methods passed**, including exhaustive all-65,536 two-byte combinations; all 256 byte values; independent transformation inverse checks; randomized lengths, random inputs, special lengths, and corruption/overwrite failures.
- **Actual H.264/AAC MP4 input:** 33,028,976 bytes, SHA-256 `615a72af951d8c4833bca80dc7a569340abdfca2b4574af9b1e72302f580b8ca`.
- **Adaptive default (1 MiB blocks, Brotli 5):** 32,514,744 bytes (1.5569% smaller), 32 blocks: 27 raw/zlib, 4 raw/Brotli, 1 store.
- **Adaptive higher quality (1 MiB blocks, Brotli 9):** 32,499,550 bytes (1.6029% smaller), 32 blocks: 14 raw/zlib, 17 raw/Brotli, 1 store.
- **Best earlier full-file reference, Brotli 9:** 32,493,332 bytes (1.6217% smaller). **This beats adaptive quality 9 by 6,218 bytes.** The adaptive wrapper and block boundaries have a cost.
- All recovered MP4 bytes match the original, and the SHA-256 matches.

All are byte-exact with matching SHA-256, including deletion of the source before the decoder runs in regression tests. Control data are deterministic, generated by `random.Random(20261123)` and are **synthetic by construction**; they do not establish a compression gain for normal video footage.

## What the outcome shows

1. Adaptive selection can choose different reversible representations for different blocks of a file; this is a working example of typed, lossless re-expression.
2. HEX 6+2 can make certain bit-level periodicities easier for standard compressors to exploit.
3. The supplied MP4 did **not** benefit from HEX preprocessing, and the adaptive archive is slightly larger than unblocked Brotli 9 on that file.
4. The reported savings are relative to an *already compressed MP4*. They are not a measure of information retained from the original camera recording and should not be called lossless video re-encoding.
5. Next meaningful comparison: diverse held-out video/raw-image files, multiple block sizes, time/memory budget, stronger baselines, and independent reproduction. Continue reporting failures.

See `benchmark_results.json` and `quality9_results.json` for machine-readable detailed measurements. The source video is not published; the scripts and synthetic controls are.
