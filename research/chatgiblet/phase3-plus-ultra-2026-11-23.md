# ChatGiblet III — Plus Ultra: spectral observability
Research exposition, 2026-11-23. Classical analytic ingredients; novelty unverified. Not a new proof of RH or GRH, nor Lean-certified. Credit for the recently announced arithmetic proof goes to Alpöge and Claude; see https://www.math.uwaterloo.ca/~mrubinst/alpoge_claude_grh/ . Classical Mellin and explicit-formula background: https://people.maths.ox.ac.uk/greenbj/papers/primenumbers.pdf .

## 1. Abstract Lp growth theorem
Let R:[1,infinity)->C be measurable and locally bounded, F(s)=int_1^infinity R(x)x^(-s-1) dx initially in a right half-plane. Assume meromorphic continuation to a connected half-plane Re s>b, with nonempty poles of finite real-part supremum Theta>b. Assume for every eps>0, R(x)=O_eps(x^(Theta+eps)). For finite p>=1 and real a define D_j=int_j^(j+1)|exp(-at)R(exp t)|^p dt. Then limsup D_j^(1/j)=exp(p(Theta-a)).
Proof: pointwise growth gives the upper bound. On any compact subset of Re s>c, Holder on each unit block bounds the absolute integral by C_K D_j^(1/p) exp(-(Re s-a)j). If root limsup were strictly smaller than exp(p(Theta-a)), select b<c<Theta with root limsup<exp(p(c-a)). The block integrals are entire, and the root test gives locally uniform convergence for Re s>c. The resulting holomorphic function agrees with F in the initial right half-plane. Meromorphic uniqueness contradicts a pole to the right of c. No maximal pole need exist. The same proof for block essential supremum N_j gives limsup N_j^(1/j)=exp(Theta-a). More generally, the upper pointwise hypothesis can be replaced by the corresponding upper block norm rate. Without the upper hypothesis the pole boundary gives only a lower bound.

## 2. Compact smoothing and pole visibility
For a finite complex measure mu on [0,H], S_mu R(x)=int R(x exp u)dmu(u). F_mu(s)=K_mu(s)F(s)-B_mu(s), with K_mu(s)=int exp(su)dmu(u) and B_mu(s)=int exp(su) int_1^(exp u) R(y)y^(-s-1)dy dmu(u), both entire. Fubini and change of variables establish the formula initially in absolute convergence, then continuation. If F has a pole of order m and K has a zero of order k at rho, the remaining pole order is max(m-k,0); for k=0 a simple pole residue is multiplied by K(rho). Zero multiplicity of zeta is distinct from pole order of its logarithmic derivative: its poles are simple with residue equal to multiplicity. Pointwise growth O_eps(x^(Theta+eps)) survives finite compact smoothing.
A single filter preserves the original Lp root rate if its visible poles have real-part supremum Theta. No conclusion of a *smaller* exact rate follows from pole cancellation alone; an improved upper bound is also required.

## 3. Filter banks
For finite measures mu_1,...,mu_N define J_j=sum_l D_j(S_l R). If for poles arbitrarily close in real part to Theta at least one filter leaves a pole, then limsup J_j^(1/j)=exp(p(Theta-a)). Proof: upper bound by finite sum; strict smaller limsup would bound every channel and force all channel Mellin transforms holomorphic in Re s>c for some c<Theta, contradicting visibility. A simple sufficient condition is no common multiplier zeros at any pole.
Universal two-channel example: unnormalized boxcar integrals over [0,1] and [0,sqrt(2)]. K_h(s)=(exp(hs)-1)/s for s!=0 and h at s=0. Their nonzero roots are 2*pi*i*k/h, k nonzero integer; no common root because sqrt(2) is irrational. Thus every pole is visible to at least one channel. For poles strictly in Re s>0 either boxcar alone suffices. The bank guarantees detectability in this asymptotic sense, not a uniform stable inversion: its responses can be arbitrarily small.
Counterexample: R(exp t)=exp(beta*t)cos(gamma*t)+exp(lambda*t), beta=.8, lambda=.6, gamma=2*pi. With h=.37, q=exp(beta*h), the real notch filter S=shift(2h)-2q cos(gamma*h)shift(h)+q^2 identity annihilates both dominant conjugate modes. Its multiplier factors into (exp(hs)-exp(h(beta+i gamma)))(exp(hs)-exp(h(beta-i gamma))). It leaves a nonzero multiple of exp(lambda*t). This is an actual loss of exponential energy growth, not a counterexample to RH.
A different limitation: R(exp t)=exp(beta*t+i exp t) has block norm rate exp(beta-a), but F(s)=int_1^infinity y^(beta-s-1)exp(iy)dy has entire continuation by repeated integration by parts. Therefore poles alone cannot characterize energy without a suitable upper-growth or explicit-formula assumption.

## 4. Dirichlet progression aggregate
Fix q>=1, phi=phi(q), reduced residue classes r. E_r(x)=sum_{n<=x,n congruent r mod q} Lambda(n)-x/phi. E_chi(x)=sum_{n<=x}Lambda(n)chi(n)-delta_chi x for all characters mod q, with delta=1 for principal. Orthogonality gives E_chi=sum_r chi(r)E_r and sum_r|E_r|^2=(1/phi)sum_chi|E_chi|^2 pointwise. Integrate exp(-t) over [j,j+1] for an exact finite-conductor energy identity. For each character the Mellin transform is -L'/ (s L)-delta/(s-1). Standard fixed-conductor explicit formulas (or the standard zero-bound error estimate) give E_chi=O_(q,eps)(x^(Theta_q+eps)), where Theta_q is the maximum rightmost nontrivial zero boundary of the primitive L-functions inducing the characters mod q. Imprimitive Euler factors contribute only O_q(log x) to the prime sums. A character with poles approaching Theta_q gives the lower bound by the abstract theorem. Thus the aggregate progression energy has root limsup exp(2Theta_q-1). This is a fixed-q criterion equivalent to the corresponding family of critical-line assertions, by functional-equation symmetry across conjugate characters; it is not a uniform estimate in q. Finite fixed banks of compact filters satisfying pole visibility can likewise be used before aggregation.
