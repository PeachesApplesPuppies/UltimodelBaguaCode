## 1. Two energy exponents
Let Theta be the supremum of the real parts of nontrivial zeta zeros, E(x)=psi(x)-x, G(x)=sum_{n<=x} Lambda(n)(x/n-1)-x log x+(1+gamma)x. Define V_j and W_j as the integrals over [j,j+1] of exp(-t) times |E(exp t)|^2 and |G(exp t)|^2 respectively.
Claim: limsup V_j^(1/j)=limsup W_j^(1/j)=exp(2Theta-1).
Upper bounds: the classical truncated explicit formula yields E(x)=O(x^Theta log^2 x), and the absolutely convergent explicit formula for G gives G(x)=O(x^Theta) (sum of |1/(rho(rho-1))| converges by zero counting). When Theta=1 the upper bound on E follows also from Chebyshev. Thus both root limsups are at most exp(2Theta-1).
Lower bounds: for R=E or G, let D_j be its energy. The Mellin transform of R initially converges for Re s>1. Each block integral is entire and has modulus <= sqrt(D_j) exp(-(Re s-1/2)j) for Re s>=1/2; more generally multiply by max(1,exp(1/2-Re s)). If limsup D_j^(1/j)<exp(2Theta-1), choose real a<Theta with limsup D_j^(1/j)<exp(2a-1). The root test gives locally uniform absolute convergence in Re s>a, even when a<1/2. The sum is holomorphic and agrees with the Mellin transform by uniqueness of analytic continuation. But at any zero rho of multiplicity m with Re rho>a, the transforms have respective nonzero residues -m/rho and -m/[rho(rho-1)]. Contradiction. No assumption that Theta is attained is needed. This argument uses standard explicit-formula and complex-analysis inputs, not the arithmetic proof.
 
## 2. Windowed positive-energy refinement
Let A(t)=exp(-t/2)G(exp t), U_j=int_j^(j+1) max(A(t),0)^2 dt, H(x)=int_0^1 G(x exp u) du for x>=1 and zero otherwise.
For t>=0, j=floor(t):
 exp(-t/2) H(exp t) <= sqrt((e-1)(U_j+U_{j+1})).
Proof: discard negative values and use Cauchy–Schwarz against exp(u/2), with squared norm e-1, then cover [t,t+1] by the two blocks.
Mellin(H)(s)=K(s)Mellin(G)(s)-B(s), K(s)=(exp(s)-1)/s, B(s)=int_0^1 exp(su) int_1^(exp u) G(y)y^(-s-1) dy du, entire. K has no zeros in Re s>0. All nontrivial poles survive. H's transform is regular at the real point Theta (zeta has no real zeros in the open critical strip, and the pole at 1 cancels). The rightmost pole supremum remains Theta. The cited KP theorem therefore applies with positive least boundary ordinate (or infinity), even if Theta is not attained. For any 0<delta<1/4, and any sufficiently large T, choose X=exp(1.4T) and alpha=0.2. There is t in [1.12T,1.4T] with H(exp t)>=exp((Theta-delta)t) by the oscillation-size conclusion of KP. Both blocks j and j+1 are contained in [T,1.5T] for large T.
At least one of U_j and U_{j+1} is >=exp((2Theta-1-2delta)t)/(2(e-1)). Since the chosen index differs from t by at most 1, its root has lower limit >= exp(2Theta-1-2delta).
Let Q(T)=max {U_j^(1/j): integer j>=1, [j,j+1] subset [T,1.5T]}. Upper bound follows from U_j<=W_j. Letting delta decrease to zero gives lim_{T->infty} Q(T)=exp(2Theta-1). This uses KP as a cited theorem, and is not a new arithmetic proof. It strengthens the window criterion in Phase I and avoids the false inference that a small smoothed function has a small derivative. The result is a literature-dependent derivation requiring independent review.
The Waterloo arithmetic input G(x)<=x^(1/2+eps) on arbitrarily late windows gives Q(T)<=exp(2eps+o(1)) there, hence Theta<=1/2; the symmetry of zeros gives equality and both original energies follow. This is still a zero-exclusion argument, not a direct global derivative estimate.
