# BCE v2.0 Multiplicative Correlation Engine
Run `python -m pip install numpy`, then `python engine.py` in this directory. Ctrl+C stops with exit code 130. Results are atomically replaced at completion; reruns recompute. No input files modified. All numbers are floating-point tests, not interval certificates. Previous v1.9 result file is included and five truncated energy values independently reproduced.

Let a_d=(-1)^(d+1)/d² and b(n)=sum_{d|n} a_d Lambda(n/d). B(x)=sum_{n<=x} b(n). Each contribution is a_d log p with d p^r<=x. The program partitions these into eight disjoint classes by d parity, p|d versus gcd(p,d)=1, and r=1 versus r>=2. Endpoint reconstruction uses independent dilation sums. For the shared-prime contribution, sum over each d and its prime divisors; each p contributes at most log x over all r, so the total absolute bound is log x times sum omega(d)/d², finite since omega(d)<=log(d)/log 2. Thus the shared contribution is O(log x). Higher powers are bounded by sum_{d<=x} d^-2 sqrt(x/d) log x <= zeta(5/2) sqrt(x) log x, since p<=sqrt(x/d) and each p contributes at most log x. Both error bounds are sufficient for finite weighted energy for all positive epsilon.

Let A(y)=sum_{d<=y} a_d, G(x)=sum_{p<=x} log p A(x/p), and c=eta(3)=3 zeta(3)/4. Then B(x)-cx = G(x)-cx + O(sqrt(x) log x). By v1.8 coercivity and the v1.3 weighted energy criterion, RH is equivalent to finite weighted energy of G(x)-cx for every epsilon>0. This eliminates higher powers and shared-divisor corrections as independently difficult terms but does not prove the remaining prime bound.

At fixed x, g_p=log p A(x/p); G²=sum g_p²+2 sum_{p<q}g_p g_q. The diagonal is O(x log²x) since |A|<=zeta(2) and at most x primes; integrated to X it is O(X² log²X). The distinct-prime cross terms in the centered square combine with -2cxG+c²x². Controlling this combined quantity is not proved. Raw cross terms are positive, not evidence of destructive interference after centering. Parity split has main terms (7/8)zeta(3)x and -(1/8)zeta(3)x, respectively. Centered parity energies are numerical trapezoidal quadratures in log x. Negative cross terms observed at five weights do not imply global bounded energy.
