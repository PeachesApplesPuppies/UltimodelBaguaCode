For integer q>=2 put a_q(k)=1-q*1_{q|k}. The divisor identity gives sum_{k<=x} a_q(k) psi(x/k)=log(x!)-q log(floor(x/q)!) for positive integer x. The zero-mean periodic weights give convergence for Re(s)>0 and multiplier D_q(s)=(1-q^(1-s))*zeta(s), with removable value log(q) at s=1. Summation by parts bounds the tail after N by (q-1)(1+|s|/Re(s))*N^(-Re(s)). Thus a hypothetical off-line zero yields a mode annihilated by every D_q, with bounded truncated error for x^s at N=floor(x). No such zero is asserted to exist.

The extra zeros of the factors for q=2 and q=3 do not coincide away from s=1: coincidence implies a nontrivial equality of powers of 2 and 3. At s=1 the removable values are nonzero. Consequently the common zeros of D_2 and D_3 in Re(s)>0 are exactly the zeta zeros there. This removes extraneous zeros, not the zeta zeros themselves. Any linear combination with holomorphic coefficients retains the common zeta factor away from s=1. No global cancellation bound or RH proof follows.
