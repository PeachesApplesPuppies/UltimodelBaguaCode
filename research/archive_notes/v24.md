## Design and results
Reopened v2.3 source, results, proof audit and metaphor registry. Regenerated prime powers to 10^7 and reproduced all six archived discrepancy endpoints (absolute tolerance 1e-8). The normalized signal is sampled at 4096 equally spaced log coordinates. Delay windows of length 256 are split into early training and late testing with a 256-window purge to prevent raw-sample overlap. Training second-moment matrix C = XX^T / m is compressed to its top r eigenvectors V and positive eigenvalues L. The metric in projected coordinates is L^(-1); whitening W=L^(-1/2)V^T yields identity training second moment. This metric is positive only on the retained subspace, not necessarily on the full original space. Fit K in whitened coordinates by unconstrained least squares; do not project to a unitary operator. Test the spectral norm of K^T K-I, the held-out relative prediction error and the relative norm of successive held-out squared-energy changes. The same training construction is used for synthetic controls. It is an uncentered second moment, not centered covariance.

## Exact theorem: finite-dimensional invariant metrics
For an invertible complex matrix K, a positive-definite Hermitian M with K*MK=M exists iff K is diagonalizable and all eigenvalues have modulus one. Necessity: M^(1/2) K M^(-1/2) is unitary. Sufficiency: K=PDP^(-1) with unitary diagonal D, so M=(P^(-1))*P^(-1) works. Reciprocal eigenvalues alone are insufficient: K=diag(2,1/2) preserves the indefinite form [[0,1],[1,0]] but no positive metric. A nontrivial Jordan block with eigenvalue 1 is another obstruction. These are analytic examples, not numerical tests.

A constructive equivalent criterion is sup over all integer n of ||K^n|| <= C. If invariant M exists, similarity to a unitary matrix gives this bound. Conversely set M_N=(1/(2N+1)) sum_{n=-N}^N (K^n)*K^n. Uniform bounds on K^n and K^(-n) imply C^(-2)I <= M_N <= C^2 I. A convergent subsequence exists in finite dimension. K*M_N K-M_N consists of the two endpoint terms divided by 2N+1, which tends to zero. Its limit is the required positive invariant metric. This is a standard finite-dimensional unitarizability argument, not a novelty claim or proof of RH. Infinite-dimensional extension requires a specified operator, topology, domain and uniform bounds; no such arithmetic realization is constructed here.
