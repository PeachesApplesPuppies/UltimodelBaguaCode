# BCE v2.3 — Four-Sided Spectral Resonance Engine

Run `python -m pip install -r requirements.txt` and `python engine.py`. Ctrl+C interrupts (native library operations may finish before Python handles the interrupt). Completed stages are atomically written to results.json; rerun recomputes rather than resumes. No input files are modified. Memory requirement is several hundred MB. Results are floating-point unless explicitly described as exact.

## Four sides
1. Arithmetic: sieve prime powers to 10^7, sample (psi(exp(t))-exp(t))/exp(t/2) at 4096 equally spaced logarithmic coordinates; Hann-window FFT, no zeta zeros supplied.
2. Operator: delay embed the same arithmetic signal, compress to dominant covariance eigenvectors, fit a shift K by least squares, project K to its unitary polar factor Q, and form a Hermitian generator using unitary Schur decomposition and principal eigenphases divided by sample spacing. Ranks 12,20,32. This is a finite data-driven candidate, not a Hilbert–Pólya operator. It shares data with the FFT, so the two are not statistically independent.
3. Analytic: calculate first ten critical-line ordinates with mpmath only after fitting. Compare the first five to each spectrum. This does not count all zeros in a region.
4. Metaphorical: see registry.md. Metaphors motivate controlled experiments, not premises. Test artificial growth at delta=0,.01,.05,.25 with frequency 14 and a bounded frequency 21 component. Unconstrained fit recovers growth; unitary projection necessarily removes growth. Exact polynomial control checks reflection and a negative Hermite form.

## Proof audit
Let Xi(w)=xi(1/2+iw). A self-adjoint H whose spectrum includes every zero of Xi (as a complex w, not merely its real part) would imply RH. To prove such a result one needs a Hilbert space, a specified dense domain, self-adjointness, and an unconditional complete spectral correspondence including multiplicity. Our finite matrices are Hermitian by construction, but they have no such completeness theorem. A fit to finitely many frequencies cannot imply completeness. In fact the nearest operator frequencies for the first five analytic ordinates are less accurate than the FFT in all three tested ranks. No proof of RH.

Exact obstruction: if K has an eigenvector v with eigenvalue exp((delta+i omega)dt), delta != 0, no positive definite metric M can satisfy K* M K=M, since evaluating on v yields (exp(2 delta dt)-1) v* M v=0. A unitary projection changes the operator rather than establishing that identity. This is the distinction between an observed resonance and a genuinely lossless instrument. The synthetic controls are not zeta counterexamples.

Exact analytic control: P(w)=((w-20)^2+1/16)((w+20)^2+1/16) is even and positive for real w, but has zeros at +/-20 +/- i/4. Real-coefficient interpolation gives a polynomial q with q(20+i/4)=i, q(20-i/4)=-i, and zero at both other roots. Sum q(root)^2=-2, rejecting Hermite positivity. Real-axis positivity, reflection, or a real fitted spectrum alone cannot exclude off-axis zeros.
